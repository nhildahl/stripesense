Questions

Straight answers.

What does "AI-driven" mean here?

Two things, both running on your own laptop: prediction formulas scored against your own runs, with a Smart Blend that weights the closest, and a neural-network text reader for your slips. It isn't a chatbot, and your runs aren't sent anywhere to be analyzed. The delay box comes from your last few runs' reactions, not from AI. Predictions are estimates, and every dial-in comes with a confidence %.

Can I change how it looks?

Yes. The next update has six looks: Classic, Neural, Pit Wall, Daylight, Obsidian and Carbon. New installs start in Obsidian, and you can switch on first-time setup, with the palette button at the top, or on App Setup. The current download has the one original look.

How much does it cost?

The 30-day trial is free, with every feature on. Pricing is being finalized. After the trial, predictions, the Lanes numbers and sending need a license key; your log and data always stay yours.

I race by myself. Does it still work?

That's who it's made for. Log your run when you get back to the trailer, and Stripe IQ can send the numbers for the next round to a pager, or to your phone, by itself whenever the weather changes them, while Stripe IQ is open.

Which parts need the internet at the track?

The predictions and slip reading run on your laptop without the internet. The pager sends the numbers over its own radio link, not the internet: two small radios on a private channel. The pager kit is coming soon. It works with the Windows version, over USB. The Android pager (over Bluetooth) is in the next update and hasn't been tested on a phone yet. Phone messages (through the free ntfy service) and online weather need a connection, like track Wi-Fi or a hotspot.

Can it read a time slip, or a ladder?

A photo of your time slip fills in both lanes. It reads the win light too, so the win or loss is filled in when the slip shows one (next update), and you can change the result if it got it wrong. The next update adds posted sheets: you can type or paste a ladder or a qualifying sheet, or scan a photo of a ladder (still being tested; reading a qualifying sheet from a photo hasn't been tested yet). Everything is read on your own device. Slips and sheets differ from track to track and photos aren't perfect: numbers it isn't sure of are highlighted, but a few mistakes can slip through, so check every row before you save. Lay the sheet flat, fill the picture and keep it sharp.

Where is my data kept?

Your runs, cars and photos stay on your own computer unless you choose to send them somewhere, and nothing is sent for analysis. Numbers you send to a pager go by radio on your private channel, and numbers you send to your phone go through the free ntfy service. Weather, track search, elevation lookups and update checks use the internet when they run. The current download also has an optional online 3D-model tool that sends car photos only if you use it; the next update removes it. You can make a backup file at any time and move it to a new laptop.
